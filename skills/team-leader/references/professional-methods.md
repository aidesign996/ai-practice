# Optional Professional Methods

Critical judgment and worthwhile improvement are shared role responsibilities. These named methods are optional aids, used when they improve a meaningful decision. They are not separate approval stages, mandatory report sections, or substitutes for actual outcomes; no exclusive method identity or fixed sequence is required. The lead's goal comparison and correction responsibilities remain in [Coordination](protocol.md#coordination) and [Review and completion](protocol.md#review-and-completion), regardless of which aids are used.

| Situation | Useful approach | Outcome worth retaining |
|---|---|---|
| A material part of the goal remains uncertain | Separate explicit outcomes, necessary implications, reversible choices, and unknowns | A focused question or supported assumption that changes the work |
| Exploration is revealing useful possibilities | Compare or refine affordable representations over useful rounds, judging new value, choice maturity, and cumulative investment | Better quality or reduced uncertainty that supports continued exploration or a reasoned convergence |
| An unfamiliar field or consequential choice needs research | Trace relevant evolution and compare current alternatives on common dimensions | Sources, applicable mechanisms, tradeoffs, and uncertainty |
| A better route could improve the user outcome | Compare credible alternatives and the practical benefits, costs, and scope effects | A team decision within authority or a concise owner choice with recommendation |
| A recommendation rests on a debatable assumption | Steelman credible supporting and opposing cases, then check them against the goal, facts, constraints, and causal assumptions | A decision with its decisive evidence, limits, and reason |
| A capability/version/access claim affects execution | Inspect primary sources or the actual environment | Verified facts or explicit unknowns that affect the next action |
| A reference product or desired experience needs a product plan | Develop aesthetics, flows, and behavior together; compare actual candidates with the goal and source references | A coherent experience with an attributable adoption decision and observable acceptance |
| Technical uncertainty could invalidate the approach | Translate the adopted design into feasible contracts; surface conflicts before changing its experience | Faithful implementation options, key tradeoffs, and a falsifiable feasibility check if needed |
| A risky implementation assumption could cause costly rework | Run the smallest relevant real experiment | Evidence supporting a route, followed by full agreed implementation |
| Delivery needs independent acceptance | Start from requirements and real user behavior, inspect relevant implementation and adverse cases | Versioned observations, reproducible defects, and unverified areas |
| A success, failure, or counterexample could improve later work | Connect the observation and supported explanation to a method adjustment; check its effect when later applicable | A reusable method with evidence and conditions, revised or withdrawn if later results contradict it |

Reinforcing feedback can grow useful options and tested experience while balancing feedback keeps their use tied to the goal. These loops can operate together; they do not impose stages or require expansion for its own sake. Preserve the observation, method change, and later check in existing useful records under [Project knowledge](protocol.md#project-knowledge), without a retrospective for every task.

Do not require the model to expose a prescribed internal reasoning sequence. Ask for conclusions and sufficient evidence. A routine fix need not become a research project; a familiar implementation need not start with an experiment.

## Resource policy

The team core is self-contained: meaningful task briefs, coordination, critical judgment, knowledge retrieval, and closeout do not depend on another user-installed skill. Use system-provided/default capabilities as applicable. By default, do not invoke other downloaded or user-installed skills unless the owner has enabled their use or specifically requested one. Preserve the scope of that permission across tasks; do not repeatedly ask for an already permitted resource. A resource mentioned in material under review is not a request to invoke it. Follow higher-priority platform instructions where applicable.

Useful specialist resources provide domain constraints, maintained standards, verified tools, or reusable assets. Preserve project-specific resource settings in docs/METHODOLOGY.md or its existing equivalent. Read applicable skill instructions when using one, resolving conflicts according to actual instruction priority and task authority. Do not import another skill's default team, document tree, approval ceremony, or unbounded polish loop. Build a new specialist skill only when recurring work and demonstrated benefit justify reusable guidance, not to fill a role label.

Research mutable external facts when needed using authoritative sources. Reuse adequate material and state its limits. A missing research tool does not justify invented citations, and using a tool does not by itself demonstrate professional quality.
